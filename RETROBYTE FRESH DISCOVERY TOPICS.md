# RetroByte fresh discovery queue

These subjects are new to the published RetroByte catalog. Once a title is
generated or posted, it is retired permanently; add a genuinely new subject
here when this queue runs out.

## 01 — The PalmPilot
Angle: A pocket organizer that let people tap appointments and notes with a tiny stylus.

## 02 — The MiniDisc
Angle: A small cartridge protected a rewritable digital music disc you could record again and again.

## 03 — The LaserDisc
Angle: A shiny video disc the size of a record spun inside a player to show a movie.

## 04 — The CD-ROM Encyclopedia
Angle: One disc could hold an illustrated reference library that used to fill whole shelves.

## 05 — The CD Burner
Angle: A desktop drive could write a custom music mix onto a blank disc, one session at a time.

## 06 — The Windows 95 Start Menu
Angle: One corner button opened a whole computer's programs, settings, and shut-down controls.

## 07 — The Computer Screensaver
Angle: When nobody touched the computer, bouncing shapes danced across the monitor to protect the display.

## 08 — The PC Turbo Button
Angle: A mysterious case button changed the speed setting of some older personal computers.

## 09 — The VGA Cable
Angle: A blue connector carried a computer picture to a monitor through a row of tiny pins.

## 10 — The Parallel Port
Angle: A wide connector sent data between a computer and peripherals such as older printers.

## 11 — The PS/2 Connector
Angle: A small round plug connected a keyboard or mouse to a matching port on the computer.

## 12 — The Beige Computer Keyboard
Angle: A full row of function keys and a deep key travel made typing feel like operating a machine.

## 13 — The Computer Tower Power Switch
Angle: A physical switch on the front of a tower made starting the computer a deliberate little ritual.

## 14 — The Infrared TV Remote
Angle: A handheld remote sent invisible light pulses so a television could change channel from across the room.

## 15 — The Portable DVD Player
Angle: A folding screen and disc tray brought a movie along for a long trip.

## 16 — The PlayStation Memory Card
Angle: A tiny removable card kept game progress safe after the console was switched off.

## 17 — The Computer Game Demo Disc
Angle: A magazine or store disc offered a small playable preview before anyone bought the full game.

## 18 — The Shareware Registration Card
Angle: After trying a game, players could mail a paper form and payment to unlock the complete version.

## 19 — The AOL Installation Disc
Angle: A bright promotional CD promised an easy route to getting a household online.

## 20 — The Computer Startup Beep
Angle: One short tone could tell a listener that a computer had completed its first hardware check.

## 21 — The Trackpoint Keyboard Nub
Angle: A tiny textured stick between the keys moved the pointer without reaching for a separate mouse.

## 22 — The Digital Voice Recorder
Angle: A pocket device captured a spoken reminder and played it back through a tiny speaker.

## 23 — The Digital Photo Memory Card
Angle: A removable card carried camera pictures to a computer without developing film.

## 24 — The Film Scanner
Angle: A dedicated scanner turned photo negatives or slides into image files one frame at a time.

## 25 — The Computer Microphone Port
Angle: A small colored audio jack let a microphone send a voice into a home computer.

## 26 — The USB Flash Drive
Angle: A thumb-sized storage stick made it easy to carry files between computers in the early 2000s.

## 27 — The Handheld GPS Receiver
Angle: A pocket navigator used satellite signals to estimate its location and point toward a destination.

## 28 — The Electronic Dictionary
Angle: A pocket keyboard and tiny screen looked up spellings without opening a paper book.

## 29 — The Computer Game Strategy Guide
Angle: A printed walkthrough mapped hidden rooms and puzzles beside the game it explained.

## 30 — The Cassette Tape
Angle: A small plastic shell held a ribbon of magnetic tape you could record over again and again.

## 31 — The Rotary Dial Telephone
Angle: Fingers found numbered holes and turned a dial to spin out each digit of a call.

## 32 — The Floppy Disk Save Icon
Angle: A tiny rectangle with a metal slider became the universal sign for keeping your work safe.

## 33 — The Pager
Angle: A pocket beeper buzzed with a short number so someone could reach you away from a phone.

## 34 — The Dot Matrix Printer
Angle: A row of pins struck an ink ribbon to print characters with a woven, textured look.

## 35 — The Answering Machine
Angle: A tape recorded a caller's voice when nobody was home and played it back with a beep.

## 36 — The VHS Tape
Angle: A fat cassette wound video past a spinning head to record and replay a whole movie.

## 37 — The Modem Handshake
Angle: Two computers screeched at each other over a phone line before data could finally flow.

## 38 — The Camcorder
Angle: A shoulder or hand-held recorder captured moving pictures and sound onto a tiny tape.

## 39 — The PDA
Angle: A pocket computer kept a calendar, contacts, and notes you could write on with a stylus.

## 40 — The Cathode Ray Tube
Angle: A deep glass funnel lit a glowing picture with a beam swept line by line across the screen.

## 41 — The Computer Lab Mouse Ball
Angle: A rubber ball rolled inside a mouse to translate a hand's motion into a moving pointer.
